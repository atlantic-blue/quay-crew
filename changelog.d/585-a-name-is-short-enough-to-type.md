**Length is the first filter on a name, and it is 3 to 6 characters.** A person types the tool's name
before every command, dozens of times a day, so a long name is a cost paid on every line. `docs/NAME.md`
now refuses anything above 6 characters on length alone. That dropped `hardstand`, `dunnage` and
`wharfinger`, which had passed every other line of the bar.

`blue quay` was refused as well, and the reason generalises: Red Hat's guidelines refuse their marks
"in whole or in part", including a takeoff or an abbreviation, so a qualifier in front of a taken word
inherits the collision rather than removing it. The heard form, `bluekey`, is a live European Union
software trademark on top of that.

The bar gained two checks that were missing. A name is now checked against the commands a developer
already has, because two letters died on `bq` belonging to Google BigQuery, and the note records the
blind spot: a tool shipped inside a vendor kit answers none of the package checks. The `.dev` address
is checked and the `.com` holder is named, because every single word `.com` is taken and that fact
never separates two names.

Sixty four short candidates were screened. One cleared every line, and two near misses are written
down so nobody screens them twice.
