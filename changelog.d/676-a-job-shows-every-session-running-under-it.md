**A person opens the sessions running under one job and sees them one line each.** A job in its test
stage holds six conversations. One is the session the job runs in. The other five are the runs its
stage fanned out into, one for each requirement.

Five of those six were only visible at the command line. The console said nothing about the runs
beside the job. A person who watched a fan out left the console to see it.

Press `s` on a job to open them. Each line gives the session, its phase, its stage, its work and its
age. The line for the job says `the job itself`. A run names what it runs, for example
`tests for requirement 3`.

Enter on one of those lines opens that conversation. It is the key the sessions listing already gives
to a conversation. Where a conversation sits beside the console, that pane becomes the one the line
names.

A run that has no session yet keeps its line. The line says `not yet`, and both keys refuse it by
name. A fan out of five never draws three lines.

What one of those conversations did is on `t`. Press it on any of the six lines.

Enter on a job still reads the job. A console with no system behind it cannot read one, so enter
there goes down into the conversations under the job instead. A job that has reached no conversation
at all is refused, and the refusal says the phase the job waits in.

Nothing new is recorded. The view reads the job and its runs, and the system already held both.
