**A job says what it understood, and a person answers, before it writes a plan.** The gate that
approves a plan has been in front of the work since 576. It stopped one step too late. The session
read the sentence, read the brief, and wrote seven steps out of what it made of the two. Nobody asked
it what the sentence meant. So the plan was the session marking its own reading. A misreading passes
that gate whole, because the steps agree with the misreading. The operator's report on the last job
through it: it asked nothing, and the crew judged its own reading with no input.

A job that states the sentence now says what it understood first. It writes what the work is. It
writes what the work is not. It lists what it does not know. It marks each footing as something it
was told or something it filled in itself. Those two read the same on a row today, and they are not
the same thing. It says how sure it is, in its own words. Nothing is compared against that number:
the person is the gate. Then it asks between one and five things it cannot answer from the
repository, the brief and the sentence, and it stops. `krewe job show` prints all of it. So does the
briefing panel.

**The answer is content, not consent.** A plan is approved by the word `yes`. This is answered in
prose, with `krewe job answer <job> "1: ..."`. Those words are kept whole. The plan is written from
them. A question the answer leaves alone stays unknown, rather than taken as agreed, and the task
that asks for the plan names it as still open. So `yes` is a valid answer here that agrees with
nothing. What the session marked as assumed is still an assumption after the answer, and it travels
into the plan with that mark.

It is the plan's own gate, one stage earlier. The same person holds it and the same test opens it: a
job that states no sentence is an errand, and a job declared under another is one part of a plan
somebody already approved. Two columns carry it, `ideation` and `ideation_answer`. There is no flag
beside them. An approval is one word and needs a flag to tell it from a refusal. Prose that is there
was written by somebody.

**What this costs, and what it does not do.** A planned job now runs two tasks before a person
approves anything. It ran one. It stops twice instead of once, so an operator who answers in the
morning pays for two nights of a container rather than one. Nothing chases them. A job waiting on
this is found with `krewe job list --phase asking`, the same as any other. The system cannot tell a
question worth asking from one whose answer is in the code. The ask says so, and the person is the
only reader who can. And a person who answers without reading has answered nothing. The plan gate has
the same hole, and no system closes it.
