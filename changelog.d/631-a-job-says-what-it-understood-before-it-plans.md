**A job says what it understood, and a person answers, before it writes a plan.** The gate that
approves a plan has been in front of the work since 576. It was one step too late. The session read
the sentence, read the brief, and wrote seven steps out of whatever it made of the two, and nobody was
ever asked what the sentence meant. So the plan was the session marking its own reading, and a
misreading passes that gate whole, because the steps agree with the misreading all the way down. The
operator's report on the last job to go through it: it asked nothing, and the crew judged its own
reading with no input.

A job that states the sentence now says what it understood first. It writes what the work is and what
the work is not, lists what it does not know, and marks each footing as something it was told or
something it filled in itself. Those two read the same on a row today and they are not the same thing.
It says how sure it is in its own words, and nothing is compared against that number: the person is
the gate. Then it asks between one and five things it cannot answer from the repository, the brief and
the sentence, and it stops. `krewe job show` prints all of it, and so does the briefing panel.

**The answer is content, not consent.** A plan is approved by the word `yes`. This is answered in
prose with `krewe job answer <job> "1: ..."`, and whatever was written is kept whole and carried into
the plan the session then writes. A question the answer leaves alone stays unknown rather than taken
as agreed, and the task that asks for the plan names it as still open. So `yes` is a valid answer here
that agrees with nothing. What was marked as assumed is still an assumption after the answer, and it
travels into the plan carrying that mark.

It is the plan's own gate one stage earlier, held by the same person and by the same test: a job that
states no sentence is an errand, and a job declared under another is one part of a plan somebody
already approved. Two columns, `ideation` and `ideation_answer`, and no flag beside them: an approval
is one word and needs a flag to be told from a refusal, while prose that is there was written by
somebody.

**What this costs, and what it does not do.** A planned job now runs two tasks before a person
approves anything, where it ran one. It stops twice instead of once, so an operator who answers in the
morning has paid for two nights of a container rather than one. Nothing chases them: a job waiting on
this is found with `krewe job list --phase asking`, the same as any other. The system cannot tell a
question worth asking from one whose answer is in the code, so the ask says so and the person is the
only reader who can. And a person who answers without reading has answered nothing, which is the same
hole the plan gate has and no system closes it.
